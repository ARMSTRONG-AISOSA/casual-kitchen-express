## 1. BRAND OVERVIEW

**Brand Name:** Casual Kitchen Express  
**Abbreviation:** CKE  
**Business Category:** Fast-Casual Food Service / Quick-Service Eatery  
**Price Positioning:** Affordable-Midrange

**Launch Location:** Ugbowo, Benin City, Edo State, close to the University of Benin  
**Delivery Range:** From Uselu to Oluku Market. CKE does not deliver beyond Oluku Market. All deliveries are handled by CKE's own riders.  
**Opening Hours:** Online orders and delivery from 8:00 am to 6:00 pm. Walk-in service until 8:30 pm.

**Business Concept:**

Casual Kitchen Express is a fast-casual food brand serving home-style Nigerian meals, made fast. Customers can order through online ordering with delivery, takeaway, or walk-in service, with an optional dine-in experience.

The business prioritizes online orders and delivery while maintaining a welcoming physical location for walk-in customers.

The long-term ambition is to establish a recognizable, operationally consistent food brand that can expand into multiple locations.

### Core Business Model

- Online food ordering (priority channel).
- Food delivery by CKE's own riders across the delivery range (priority channel).
- Takeaway orders.
- Walk-in purchases, entered at the counter and paid for in cash or online.
- Optional dine-in experience.

## 2. BUSINESS POSITIONING

**Positioning Statement:**

Home-style Nigerian meals, prepared consistently and delivered fast.

**Brand Differentiator:**

CKE combines two things that are rarely found together at an accessible price: the **authentic, home-style taste** of Nigerian cooking and **genuinely fast service**. Customers should feel they are eating food that tastes like home, without the wait.

- **Authentic taste:** Recipes and flavours that feel familiar, comforting, and true to home cooking.
- **Speed:** A focused menu and streamlined kitchen workflow that support fast preparation and fast delivery.

### Brand Value Proposition

- Authentic, home-style Nigerian meals.
- Fast preparation and delivery.
- Consistent food quality and portion sizes.
- Efficient ordering experience.
- Attractive and functional food packaging.
- Dependable takeaway and delivery services.
- A welcoming and accessible food experience.

The brand serves a broad customer base. **Online and delivery customers are the top priority**, and at launch they are expected to be mainly **students and young working adults** living and working around Ugbowo, Oluku, and Uselu. The remaining segments are listed in order of focus.

| 2 | Students and young adults | Affordability, variety, convenience |

**Primary Customer Experience Priority:** Convenient, reliable ordering and fast delivery of food that tastes like home.

## 6. BRAND COLOUR IDENTITY

**Selected Direction: Tomato Red + Golden Yellow + Warm Cream + Charcoal**

This is the adopted working colour palette for Casual Kitchen Express.

| Colour | HEX Code | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Tomato Red | `#C83F2D` | Primary brand colour |
| Golden Yellow | `#F2B544` | Secondary brand colour |
| Warm Cream | `#FFF3DF` | Background and neutral colour |
| Charcoal | `#292522` | Supporting colour and typography |

### Colour Roles

**Tomato Red — #C83F2D**
- Primary brand recognition colour.
- Used for logo applications, prominent branding, signage, and important visual elements.
- Communicates energy and food appeal.

**Golden Yellow — #F2B544**
- Secondary brand colour.
- Used for highlights, accents, promotional materials, and supporting graphics.
- Adds warmth and visual excitement.

**Warm Cream — #FFF3DF**
- Background and neutral colour.
- Used for packaging, menus, website backgrounds, and supporting surfaces.
- Creates a warm, welcoming appearance.

**Charcoal — #292522**
- Supporting colour.
- Used for typography, menu information, signage details, and visual contrast.
- Balances the brighter colours.

### Colour Usage Rules

- **Golden Yellow is used for accents and backgrounds only. It is never used for text on Warm Cream**, because the contrast is too low to read comfortably. Text on cream should use Charcoal or Tomato Red.
- Yellow text is acceptable only on Charcoal.
- Tomato Red text on Warm Cream passes contrast for all sizes. On the carton colour below, Tomato Red is used for large text and graphics only, and small text uses Charcoal.

## 8. LOGO

**Logo structure:** icon plus wordmark.

**The icon:** a cooking pot leaning slightly forward, drawn with a warm, handcrafted line. It combines the two ideas behind the brand:

- **Home cooking:** the pot, with a yellow band, handles, and three steam wisps.
- **Speed:** the forward lean and three speed lines trailing behind the pot.

**The wordmark:** "Casual Kitchen" in a soft, warm serif (Fraunces Bold), with "EXPRESS" beneath in a rounded sans (Nunito ExtraBold) with wide letter spacing. The wordmark is artwork and is not part of the typography system.

### Lockups and Backgrounds

| Background | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Warm Cream | Charcoal outlines, red pot, yellow band and lid, charcoal name, red "EXPRESS" |
| Charcoal | Cream outlines, red pot, yellow band and lid, cream name, yellow "EXPRESS" |
| Tomato Red | Cream pot with red band, yellow lid, cream name and "EXPRESS" |
| Golden Yellow | Red pot with cream lid, charcoal name and "EXPRESS" |

A horizontal lockup (icon beside the name) and a stacked lockup (icon above the name) are both approved.

### App Icon

The full icon is placed on a rounded square in red, charcoal, or yellow. One-colour versions (charcoal on cream, cream on red) are used for stamps and single-colour print.

### Usage Rules

- **Clear space:** half the icon height on every side. Nothing enters this zone.
- **Minimum size on screen:** full lockup 140 px wide, full icon 32 px.
- **Below 32 px** (down to 16 px, such as favicons), use the simplified small-size icon: the pot, lid, handles, and two steam wisps, without the speed lines or fine outlines. Testing showed the full icon becomes illegible below about 32 px.
- **Please don't:** stretch or squash the logo, change the brand colours, use yellow text on cream, or place the logo on busy backgrounds.

### Production Files

The logo is delivered as a package of production files for the app, website, and print artwork:

- Vector (SVG) and transparent PNG versions of the horizontal and stacked lockups on all four brand backgrounds, plus single-colour versions.
- The full icon and the simplified small-size icon.
- App icons (1024, 512, 192, 180 pixels, and a maskable version), favicons (SVG, ICO, PNG), and a 1080 px social profile image.
- Brand tokens (colours, fonts, type scale) as CSS and JSON, and a website head snippet and web manifest.
- All wordmark lettering is converted to outlines, so no fonts are needed to display the logo.

## 11. PLATFORM AND ORDERING OVERVIEW

CKE runs on three apps and a website. This section gives the high-level picture. The details are in `architecture.md` and `design.md`.

### The Platform

| Part | Used by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Customer app | Customers | Browse, order, pay, and track |
| Business and Kitchen app | Kitchen staff, dispatch officers, account officers, and managers (on a tablet and on phones) | Take and prepare orders (including walk-in orders at the counter), coordinate deliveries, keep the money records, and manage the menu, staff, and settings |
| Rider and Delivery app | CKE riders | Pick up and deliver orders |
| Website | Visitors | Promotes the business and mainly the app, and hosts the app download |

The apps are Android apps (Android 10 and above). For now they are installed directly from the website rather than through an app store.

All four are in development. The screens of all three apps are built and being reviewed, and clickable prototypes of every screen serve as the visual reference.

### Design Approach

Each part is designed for its job:

- **Customer app and website:** expressive and appetising, to make people want to order.
- **Kitchen and manager screens:** practical first and polished second, because they are used in a busy kitchen.
- **Rider app:** purely functional and light on the phone's battery, because riders are on bikes, in traffic, under the sun.

Across all of them, every task takes as few taps as possible.

### Ordering Experience

- The menu is open to everyone, with no sign-up needed to browse.
- Customers sign in with Google at checkout, add a delivery address (and an optional gender) the first time, then pay online by card or bank transfer.
- A repeat order takes two taps.
- Cancelling takes a reason and a confirmation, so it isn’t done by accident. Customers can deactivate their account, or delete it after a deliberate confirmation.
- Online ordering and delivery run from 8:00 am to 6:00 pm. Walk-in customers can order at the counter until 8:30 pm, and pay in cash or online.
- Customers can follow their order from the moment the kitchen acknowledges it, through preparation, rider assignment, pickup, and arrival, to delivery. A 4-digit code, shown on their tracking screen, confirms delivery.

### Delivery Model

- All deliveries are made by CKE's own riders across the delivery range, from Uselu to Oluku Market. Addresses beyond that are not accepted for delivery, and pickup is always available.
- Delivery costs a flat ₦1,500 per order on top of the food by default, and pickup has no delivery fee. Managers can change the delivery fee and rider pay, and the apps show the new amounts straight away.
- Ready orders appear in a queue, and the first rider to accept an order takes it.
- A rider carries one order at a time by default, and up to three when the orders are small enough.

### Kitchen and Management

- Kitchen staff acknowledge each order, prepare it, and mark it ready, following the packaging rules.
- Kitchen staff also enter walk-in orders at the counter, taking payment in cash or online. Managers can view and filter online and walk-in orders separately, and see sales by channel and payment method.
- Managers create rider and staff accounts, manage menu items, prices, and images, set opening hours, and can pause new orders.
- Managers can deactivate staff and manager accounts (except the owner’s), and rank customers by spend and number of orders.
- A dispatch officer coordinates the kitchen, riders, and customers: they can contact customers, see where riders are, hand an order to a rider, and settle cancellation requests.
- An account officer keeps the money records: reports, rider pay, a monthly statement, the daily cash-up, an audit trail, and closing each month.
- Every staff member and every customer has a unique ID. Staff IDs let each order be traced to who acknowledged it, who prepared it, and which rider collected and delivered it.
- Images are compressed on the device before upload, to keep storage small.
- Managers can see current and historic data: the full order list for any date range, order volumes, sales amounts, the time each order was placed, and preparation and delivery times, by day, week, month, rider, and area.

## 12. SOCIAL MEDIA VISUAL DIRECTION

**Priority platforms:** Instagram and TikTok, supported by WhatsApp Business status and Facebook.

### Content Pillars

| Pillar | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Food hero | Appetising close-ups of signature dishes, led by jollof rice |
| Arrives fast | Packed bags, riders, and delivery moments that prove the speed promise |
| Menu and prices | Clear, simple posts for dishes, combos, and prices |
| Behind the kitchen | Cooking, packing, and the people behind the food |
| Customer favourites | Reposted photos and feedback from customers |

### Photography Direction

- Warm natural light, shot overhead or at a slight angle.
- Food shown in CKE packaging as well as on plates, so the carton colour and red print become familiar.
- Visible steam and fresh, generous portions.
- Props limited to cream, charcoal, and kraft surfaces so the palette stays consistent.
- Food that looks real: natural portions and plating, with no staged or over-styled looks.

### Food Imagery

Food images are sourced from the internet for the design and build.

- **Source:** the best-quality photo available for each dish. Free-to-use libraries (such as Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, and Wikimedia Commons) are preferred, and the source of each image is recorded, with credit where the licence asks for it.
- **Subject:** Nigerian dishes that match the menu, accurate in colour, texture, and portion.
- **AI fallback:** when no suitable photo is found, AI-generated images may be used. They must be based on real-world reference photos and look as human-made as possible, with natural light, believable portions and plating, natural imperfections, and no distorted food, hands, or garbled text.

### Visual Rules

- Backgrounds are Warm Cream, Tomato Red, Golden Yellow, or Charcoal.
- Headlines use Nunito ExtraBold. Supporting text uses Inter. Prices use Inter Bold.
- Text colours follow the colour rules: Charcoal or Tomato Red on cream, cream on red, charcoal on yellow, and cream or yellow on charcoal. Yellow is never used for text on cream.
- The icon appears in a corner of each post, respecting clear space. The full lockup is used on covers and ads.
- One idea per post, with a one-line headline and a short supporting line.

### Templates

| Template | Format | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Menu feature | Square post | Cream background, food photo, dish name in Nunito, price in Inter Bold, icon in the corner |
| Promo | Square post | Yellow background, charcoal headline, red price tag |
| Story | Vertical | Tomato Red background, cream headline, tagline at the bottom |
| Reel cover | Vertical | Food photo with a cream headline strip and the icon |

### Profile and Highlights

- **Profile image:** the app icon on a Tomato Red rounded square.
- **Highlight covers:** cream circles with a single red line icon for each pillar (menu, delivery, kitchen, favourites).

### Voice

Warm, short, and direct, like a friendly neighbour who cooks. Plain dish names, light humour, and no slang overload. The tagline is used as a sign-off occasionally, not on every post.

## 13. BRAND DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES

The following principles should guide future business and brand decisions:

1. **Authenticity:** Food should taste like real, home-style Nigerian cooking.
2. **Speed:** The menu and operations should support quick preparation and delivery.
3. **Consistency:** Customers should receive consistent food quality, portions, and presentation.
4. **Convenience:** Ordering, collection, and delivery should be straightforward.
5. **Accessibility:** Pricing should remain within the affordable-midrange positioning.
6. **Recognition:** Branding should be memorable and consistent across physical and digital channels.
7. **Scalability:** Products, recipes, packaging, and operating procedures should eventually be standardized for expansion.
